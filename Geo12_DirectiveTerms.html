Directive Terms

These question verbs carry more marks in Geography writing than in most other subjects — misreading "evaluate" as "describe" is one of the most common ways students lose marks. Work through the glossary, drill the flashcards, then test yourself.

Essay writing strategy

How to actually use directive terms once you're writing an extended response — annotate the question, structure the answer, and write for a real marker.

CUBE — annotate the question before you write

  • C — Circle the directive term (e.g. evaluate, assess, discuss).
  • U — Underline the syllabus link/content focus in the question.
  • B — Bold(en) any plurals (e.g. "TWO strategies") — these tell you how many examples/points you must cover.
  • E — Elaborate with evidence and examples throughout your response.

DAWSS — what successful HSC writing does

  • D — Directive first. Answer the directive term explicitly — don't bury it.
  • A — Answer the actual question asked.
  • W — WOW detail — quantify and qualify your evidence (specific stats, named data, not vague generalisation).
  • S — Structured and logical, referencing the question/stimulus booklet.
  • S — Sustained — keep the quality and judgement going across the whole response, not just the opening.
"Evaluate" means make a judgement. You must judge effectiveness or success against stated or implied criteria — not just describe. E.g. "Evaluate the effectiveness of TWO strategies to improve people's quality of life" requires naming criteria (cost, reach, sustainability) and judging each strategy against them.

Paragraph and essay structure

  • Intro — definitions, reference to the question/stimulus booklet, and the directive term.
  • Body paragraphs — each one follows Directive → QB (question/stimulus booklet) → PEE (Point, Evidence, Explain).
  • Conclusion — restate the directive term and make a genuine synthesis/judgement, not just a summary.
Pacing: a 20-mark response should take about 35 minutes and run to roughly 5–6 pages of extended writing.

If you do nothing else: write the directive term's definition at the top of your writing-booklet page. It keeps you focused while writing, and shows the marker you know exactly what's being asked.

Write for a human marker

  • Book-end your directive terms — state them near the start and echo them again at the end.
  • Use your class learning, but also draw on your own wider geographical knowledge.
  • Leave no blank spaces on the page.
  • Plan first — even under timed conditions.
  • Adapt your existing course knowledge to whatever the specific question requires, rather than reproducing a memorised generic answer.
