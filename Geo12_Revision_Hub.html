How to revise: what actually moves your mark

Not all revision methods are equally effective. Don't over-invest in Lower Impact activities at the expense of Highest Impact ones as the exam gets closer.

Lower impact — build foundations

  • Flashcards for key terms
  • Mind maps
  • Topic summaries (1 page per dot point)

Higher impact — deepen recall

  • Past paper practice (timed)
  • Syllabus dot point checks — explain each one aloud or in writing
  • Peer teaching
  • Geographical skills practice with past papers

Highest impact — prioritise these

  • Essay planning and writing under timed conditions
  • Case study mastery sheets (location, stats, processes, strategies, evaluation)
  • Mock exams with feedback

How this hub fits together

You, here's how to get the most out of this hub:

  • Start with Directive Terms — knowing what "evaluate", "analyse" and "assess" actually want from you affects almost every mark in this course.
  • Work through each topic page in order: read the dot point notes, drill the flashcards, then move to written practice once you know the content.
  • Written practice on each topic page uses real 2025 HSC, Sample, CSSA and Independent trial questions (non-stimulus only). It times you, lets you self-mark against the real marking guideline, and archives a question once you've nailed it — so you're always practising what you're still shaky on.
  • Q16 (Skills) is where maps/graphs/images questions live, grouped by skill and difficulty, plus step-by-step guides for constructing the graphs and maps you may be asked to build.
  • Q17 (Geographical Investigation) covers fieldwork and investigation skills that can turn up as their own extended-response question.
